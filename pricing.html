Frequently asked questions

Can my school create a shared database today?

Not yet. The current accounts are private to individual educators. Institution-wide workspaces will require school organisations, properly restricted staff access, secure student-record sharing and separate pricing.

Can I use HelloEarth without paying?

Yes. The Free plan lets you keep up to three students and up to ten new lesson notes per month, with a maximum of thirty saved notes.

Can I upgrade or pay today?

Not yet. We're preparing secure card subscriptions. Paid prices are shown for planning only. Contact HelloEarth to join the launch list.

Will I lose student data if I reach my limit?

No. Existing records stay in your private account. Once a limit is reached, new inserts are blocked until more capacity is available. We won't automatically delete notes to make space.

Can I pay from another country?

We plan to support educators internationally. The paid USD plans are still previews, and checkout will open when worldwide card billing is tested.

Can I connect my own AI provider on the Free plan?

Yes, that is the intended design: all tiers, including Free, will support connecting your own compatible AI API once this feature launches. Different providers use different protocols, so we will support major providers and configurable compatible endpoints progressively, not promise every proprietary API works immediately. Your provider bills you separately. Keep API secrets out of public website code and student notes.

Which plans include cloud Atlas?

Growth and Studio are the planned cloud-hosted Atlas tiers. Rather than a separate optional add-on, each tier is designed to include a defined monthly cloud AI allowance; Studio would have the larger cap. Usage limits and final pricing must be confirmed before paid checkout opens. Hosted Atlas is not live yet.

Are recordings or unlimited AI generation included?

No. Recordings and website AI generation are not currently provided. Managed AI usage will have hard limits and clear pricing rather than an unlimited promise.
